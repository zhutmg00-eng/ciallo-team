AbstractThe use of artificial intelligence (AI) in advertising has increased significantly in recent years. In particular, generative AI enables companies to create advertising content quickly and efficiently. Despite its advantages, concerns remain about consumer trust and perceived authenticity of AI-generated advertisements, particularly in the beauty industry, where cultural differences may shape consumer responses. This study investigates how consumers perceive AI-generated versus human-generated advertising in the beauty industry and examines whether these perceptions differ between consumers in Germany and China. A quantitative research approach was applied using an online survey with a between-subjects experimental design. Participants were randomly assigned to view a beauty advertisement labelled either as AI-generated or human-created. In total, 191 valid responses from participants in Germany and China were analysed. The results show no significant differences between AI-generated and human-generated advertisements regarding consumer trust and perceived authenticity. Furthermore, no significant cultural differences were found in the effect of advertisement type. However, perceived authenticity was strongly and positively related to consumer trust, and higher levels of trust were associated with stronger purchase intention. These findings suggest that the source of advertisement creation may be less important than the overall quality and credibility of the advertising content. The study contributes to the growing literature on AI-generated advertising by providing insights into consumer responses in the beauty industry and highlighting the importance of authenticity and trust in shaping purchase intentions.
